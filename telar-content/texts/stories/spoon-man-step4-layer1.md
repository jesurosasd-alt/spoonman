How can Nazca imagery help us understand their intended purpose? The imagery of the feline mask can be related to the masks and performers in Nazca ritual (Townsend, 1985). When looking into the religion of Nazca peoples, Carmichael makes note of several "cults" which each correspond to a supernatural (Carmichael, 2016). When relating the imagery on our Nazca ceramic and the spotted cat being of Nasca cults there is a direct connection. The same whiskered face and spotted body are similar and can be believed to represent the supernatural being. With the imagery understood and the context explained it is fair to believe that the vessel in question was painted to represent the performers who were performing rituals for the spotted cat deity and using the ceramic vessel to artistically represent the being of worship. The rituals performed for these deities can be linked back to the original ancestors and suggest the original ancestors were merged as real people into the masked being that was worshipped as a supernatural being of fertility and water (Carmichael, 2016). This can further correlate with the actual performers of the rituals being masked in costumes to honor the respective deities and the notion that the painted representations on Nazca ceramics would have been made of and for ritual function as well as ritual representation.
