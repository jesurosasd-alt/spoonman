The Spotted Cat is one of the most studied mythological themes in Nasca ceramic art, with direct antecedents in south coast textile traditions and the earlier Paracas style of the Early Horizon. (Carmichael 2016) The figure is a deity: its small ears, stripped tail, semi-moon markings on the body, and spotted coat identify the pampas cat, while the upswept whiskers, though conventionally associated with the river otter, have been recontextualized within feline iconography to produce a hybrid motif linking both land and aquatic domains. (Carmichael 2016) 
 
This dual association is significant: the pampas cat evokes terrestrial fertility, while the otter signals water and rivers, together suggesting a deity that rules both agricultural and water abundance. The vegetation held beneath the figure's paw or crossed over it further reinforces its role as a fertility deity. (Silverman 1993, 230) 
 
Double-spouted vessels bearing this motif were among the most prestigious ceramic forms during Nasca phases 1 – 3, with only one per grave, signaling high status. However, their presence declines by phase 8. (Silverman 1993, 230)
