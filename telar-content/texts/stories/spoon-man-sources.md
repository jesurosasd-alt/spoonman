---
title: "Sources"
---

Kevin Vaughn's research into Nazca polychrome ceramics indicate that ceramics were distributed for religious and social purposes not for commercial trading purposes. Analysis has shown that 84% of sampled ceramics from the Nazca region share the same chemical signatures, suggesting that ceramics were likely made in a central, common area as shown in Figure 1 (Vaughn and Neff 2004). The ceremonial center of Cahuachi is a likely source of these ceramics because of its access to other production factors and regions for the distribution.

One model suggests this distribution occurred via ritual pilgrimages, where people brought local goods to Cahuachi and exchanged them for specialized clay. These ceramics served as the primary medium for Nasca ideology. According to Lidio Valdez, the nearby Acari Valley, Nasca pottery did not replace local traditions but appeared as exotic luxury goods (Valdez 2009). Valdez argues that local elites in Acari likely acquired these pieces to consolidate personal prestige.

Rather than an organized Nazca trade system, the movement of these objects likely relied on social connections, including inter-valley marriages and elite status-seeking. While the production was highly specialized and centralized in Cahuachi, the distribution appears to have been social and religious in nature linked to the prestige of Cahuachi rather than a secular commercial market.
